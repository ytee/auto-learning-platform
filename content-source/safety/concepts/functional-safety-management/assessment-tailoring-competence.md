## Learning objectives

- Lead functional-safety assessment and finding resolution without weakening independent judgment.
- Justify tailoring or alternative methods from safety objectives and equivalent confidence.
- Build role-based competence across multidisciplinary and supplier teams.
- Communicate safety trade-offs and uncertainty to different stakeholders.

## Concept

Safety leadership combines independent challenge, objective tailoring, competence development and clear stakeholder decisions. An assessment tests whether the safety argument and evidence are convincing; management responds by investigating safety impact and systemic causes rather than negotiating findings away.

Tailoring begins with the objective of an applicable requirement. The project documents context, rationale, alternative method, evidence, independent evaluation and conditions for validity. Agile delivery, commercial pressure or prior success do not by themselves justify omission.

Competence is role-based and demonstrated through education, experience, supervised work and quality of decisions. Leaders create an environment where engineers can escalate concerns and where executives, technical teams and assessors receive the same core facts expressed at the right level.

## Why it matters

Processes fail when independence is nominal, competence is assumed from job title or tailoring becomes a schedule shortcut.

Leadership quality determines whether uncertainty and bad news reach the release decision in time.

## Inputs

- Assessment plan, safety case, work products and finding criteria.
- Applicable process objectives and proposed tailoring rationale.
- Role descriptions, competence needs and team/supplier capability.
- Open risks, architecture trade-offs and stakeholder decisions.
- Organizational safety policy and escalation mechanisms.

## Activities

- Plan competent and sufficiently independent assessment participation.
- Investigate findings for product and systemic safety impact.
- Document tailoring objective, alternative evidence and approval.
- Assess competence and close gaps through training, mentoring and supervised work.
- Protect anomaly escalation and independent technical judgment.
- Communicate facts, assumptions, uncertainty and decisions by stakeholder need.

## Outputs and evidence

- Assessment findings, closure evidence and systemic corrective actions.
- Approved tailoring and alternative-method records.
- Role-based competence matrix and development evidence.
- Decision and escalation records.
- Stakeholder communication supporting accountable release governance.

## Automotive example

An assessor identifies incomplete traceability even though all tests pass. The leader distinguishes missing administrative links from missing requirements or tests, performs semantic reconciliation and targeted re-verification, corrects the process cause and updates the safety case. Release is based on closure evidence rather than argument with the assessor.

When a supplier withholds proprietary details, leadership uses the DIA to obtain adequate assurance through controlled review or independent reports while clearly recording remaining limitations.

## Common mistakes

- Treating assessment as a document audit to be passed.
- Using tailoring to remove difficult activities without equivalent confidence.
- Equating training certificates with demonstrated competence.
- Allowing managers to overrule technical findings without recorded evidence.
- Presenting different facts to executives, engineers and assessors.
